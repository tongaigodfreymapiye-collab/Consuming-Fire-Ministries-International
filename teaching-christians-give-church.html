Should Christians Give to the Church?

By Apostle T. G. Mapiye

It is very important to answer the question, “Should Christians give to the Church?” Are there logical and Scriptural reasons why we must give to the Church?

The Church is not merely a building with chairs inside and a pulpit. The Church is composed of people who share a common faith and meet regularly for fellowship and edification. The Church is a family of believers in the Lord Jesus Christ.

I believe the problem in the modern Church is not whether Christians should give, but rather what they should give and how they should give.

1. We Must Give to the Church to Support the Work of God

“Soon after this, Jesus was going through towns and villages, telling the good news about God's kingdom. His twelve apostles were with him, and so were some women who had been healed of evil spirits and all sorts of diseases. One of the women was Mary Magdalene, who once had seven demons in her. Joanna, Susanna, and many others had also used what they owned to help Jesus and his disciples. Joanna's husband Chuza was one of Herod's officials.” (Luke 8:1–3)

The Church has many expenses. These include rent, decorations, advertising materials, equipment repairs and servicing, fuel, wages for hired instrumentalists, replacement of damaged items, and consumables such as Cobra cleaning products, toiletries, hygiene supplies, food, water, and drinks. These expenses are necessary for the day-to-day running of Church affairs.

2. We Must Give to the Church to Support the Servants of God Financially

“Do you not know that those who are employed in the temple service get their food from the temple, and those who serve at the altar share in the sacrificial offerings? In the same way, the Lord commanded that those who proclaim the gospel should get their living by the gospel.” (1 Corinthians 9:13–14)

The financial support of ministers is a very important responsibility of the Church. A minister of the Gospel should not be expected to live in poverty simply because he or she is in ministry.

Every Christian has a responsibility to support those who teach them the Word of God through their financial contributions. It is not wrong for a minister to work in a secular job, run a business, or undertake other income-generating projects to support himself or herself and the family, particularly while waiting for the Church to become financially able to provide full support.

However, the Church must never evade its responsibility to support ministers, even when those ministers are capable of supporting themselves. The Bible teaches, “Don't muzzle an ox while it is threshing grain” (Deuteronomy 25:4).

“Church leaders who do their job well deserve to be paid twice as much, especially if they work hard at preaching and teaching. It is just as the Scriptures say, ‘Don't muzzle an ox when you are using it to grind grain.’ You also know the saying, ‘Workers are worth their pay.’” (1 Timothy 5:17–18)

The Apostle Paul further explained this principle using three examples:

“Who serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard without eating any of its fruit? Or who tends a flock without getting some of the milk?” (1 Corinthians 9:7)

Paul likened a minister of the Gospel to a soldier, a farmer, and a shepherd. Just as these workers benefit from the work they perform, ministers of the Gospel should receive financial support from those who benefit from their ministry.

“For it is written in the Law of Moses, ‘You shall not muzzle an ox when it treads out the grain.’ Is it for oxen that God is concerned? Does he not speak entirely for our sake? It was written for our sake, because the plowman should plow in hope and the thresher thresh in hope of sharing in the crop. If we have sown spiritual things among you, is it too much if we reap material things from you?” (1 Corinthians 9:9–11)

Everyone who dedicates himself or herself to preaching the Gospel has a right to receive support, including support for the family, while engaged in this work. Ministers of the Gospel deserve to be paid because they are engaged in God's work. While labouring in the ministry of the Word, they also have families to support.

It is therefore the responsibility of the Church to ensure that its ministers are adequately paid or otherwise fully supported.

3. We Must Give to the Church to Support Church Activities

Some Church programmes and activities require financial resources. For example, hosting an evangelistic crusade, organising a conference, or inviting a guest minister requires proper budgeting and financial planning.

When a Church has members who are generous and willing to give, such programmes become easier to organise and administer. Giving enables the Church to fulfil its ministry, reach more people, and carry out activities that advance the work of God.

4. We Must Give to the Church to Finance the Purchase of Equipment

Churches need various types of equipment, including speakers, guitars, keyboards, drums, cables, and other sound and musical equipment.

These items can be expensive and require careful financial planning. The generous contributions of Church members make it possible to purchase, maintain, and replace the equipment needed for effective worship and ministry. Through collective giving, the Church can acquire the resources necessary to serve its members and the wider community.

5. We Must Give to the Church to Meet Needs as They Arise

As a Church grows, new needs emerge, and it may become necessary to obtain funding from Church resources to address them.

Other needs arise because of unexpected challenges, changing circumstances, or the expansion of ministry activities. The resulting costs must be met, and the Church needs adequate financial resources to respond effectively.

When members give faithfully and generously, the Church is better equipped to meet these needs and continue fulfilling its responsibilities.

Conclusion

Giving to the Church is an important way of supporting the work of God, providing for His servants, financing ministry activities, purchasing essential equipment, and meeting needs as they arise. Church members should therefore approach giving with understanding, generosity, and a willingness to contribute towards the growth and effectiveness of the ministry.
