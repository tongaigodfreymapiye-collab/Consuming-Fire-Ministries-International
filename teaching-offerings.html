Offerings and Sacrifices in the Bible

What were they?

By Apostle T.G. Mapiye

Many people today in Churches use the word “offering” without fully understanding the correct meaning of the word. This has led to so many questions and a lot of confusion in churches regarding the issue of offerings. The word used for offering in the Old Testament is the Hebrew word, “qorbân” which means something brought near the altar, that is, a sacrificial present: - oblation, that is offered, offering. There are two words used for the word sacrifice, the word “ôlâh” and the word “zebach”. The word ôlâh means a step or (collectively stairs, as ascending); usually a holocaust (as going up in smoke): - ascent, burnt offering (sacrifice), go up to. The Hebrew word normally translated "sacrifice" (zebach) does not occur in Leviticus 1-3 until 3:1 in the introduction to the "peace offering". The word zebach means the flesh of an animal; by implication a sacrifice (the victim or the act). In this article the word "offering" will be used as a comprehensive term including both grain and animal offerings. "Sacrifice" will refer only to animal offerings. It is important to read the Bible with these definitions in mind. In the Old Testament there were voluntary offerings and sacrifices and involuntary offerings and sacrifices.

The word for "sacrifice" (zebah) first occurs in Genesis 31:54 in the covenant-making ceremony between Jacob and Laban: "and Jacob offered a sacrifice in the hill country and called his kinsmen to eat bread. They ate bread and spent the night in the hill country." We also read, “So Israel took his journey with all that he had and came to Beersheba, and offered sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac.” (Gen 46:1 ESV).

There are five main types of offerings in the Old Testament. The burnt offering (Leviticus 1; 6:8–13; 8:18-21; 16:24), the grain offering (Leviticus 2; 6:14–23), the peace offering (Leviticus 3; 7:11–34), the sin offering (Leviticus 4; 5:1–13; 6:24–30; 8:14–17; 16:3–22), and the trespass offering (Leviticus 5:14–19; 6:1–7; 7:1–6). Each of these offerings were fulfilled by Christ. This means that a Christian is not required by God to offer these offerings although every Christian will need to support the work of God financially in some measure to contribute to God’s work.

"For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed. Even from the days of your fathers ye are gone away from mine ordinances, and have not kept them. Return unto me, and I will return unto you, saith the LORD of hosts. But ye said, Wherein shall we return? Will a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me. But ye say, Wherein have we robbed thee? In tithes and offerings. Ye are cursed with a curse: for ye have robbed me, even this whole nation. Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.  And I will rebuke the devourer for your sakes, and he shall not destroy the fruits of your ground; neither shall your vine cast her fruit before the time in the field, saith the LORD of hosts. And all nations shall call you blessed: for ye shall be a delightsome land, saith the LORD of hosts.” (Mal 3:6-12 KJV)

It is important to see that God spoke to the sons of Jacob about abandoning His ordinances, just as their ancestors had done in the past. When we see this, we can see clearly where to classify tithes and offerings. Tithes and offerings were ordinances. In the New Testament, Christians are not commanded to keep ordinances as the Israelites were supposed to do, however, the work of God still need contributions in a meaningful and systematic way for God’s work to progress. Children of God should not abandon meeting basic needs of the church where they their spiritual needs are met.

“Neither will I any more remove the foot of Israel from out of the land which I have appointed for your fathers; so that they will take heed to do all that I have commanded them, according to the whole law and the statutes and the ordinances by the hand of Moses.” (2Ch 33:8 KJV) 
“For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.” (Joh 1:17 KJV)  Statutes and ordinances that came through Moses are different from grace and truth that came through Jesus Christ. “Having abolished in his flesh the enmity, even the law of commandments contained in ordinances; for to make in himself of twain one new man, so making peace;” (Eph 2:15 KJV) “Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;” (Col 2:14 KJV) It is right here people of God that if tithes and offerings were ordinances, then they were abolished in the flesh of Jesus Christ, they were blotted out, they were taken out of the way and they were nailed at the cross. “In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first one obsolete. And what is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away.” (Heb 8:13 ESV) The old covenant was made obsolete or it expired or it was fulfilled and then the New Testament is now in place. The priesthood changed and also the law changed and this means Christians are nolonger under obligation to keep the ordinances of the Old covenant. “For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is necessarily a change in the law as well.” (Heb 7:12 ESV) While Christians a free from keeping ordinances of the Old Testament and giving all the sacrifices and offerings that were given by the children of Israel, they still have an obligation to be generous and to support the work of God.

While tithing is no longer mandatory to keep God’s laws, it can be beneficial as a practice done voluntarily than as a requirement done to avoid the curse and earn the blessings. Malachi’s 3:8 mentions tithes and offerings and I have challenged some to give me a list of tithes and offerings in the Old Testament to check if they fully know what God was rebuking in Malachi chapter 3 and they can hardly give me a reasonable list of tithes and offerings they know. When we study the Bible we can know how GOD defined those “tithes and offerings” and that the LEVITE NATION of PRIESTS were robbing from God in Malachi 3. Those “tithes and offerings” were just as lawful as the other ordinances. If people insist that a Christian must give tithes basing on Malachi then they must be in a position to impose the offerings also. God never made tithes to be more important than the offerings. If people should decide to give tithes, such acts must be done generously and cheerfully and not in response to the rebuke in Malachi chapter 3.

It is not a surprise that some people now question the foundations of tithing and feel confused. There are two groups of people in the churches today; one group believes that Christians who do not tithe are robbing God and the second group believes that they give under grace. Tithing advocates attempt to use Malachi chapter 3 to compel every believer to tithe. This sometimes is met with resistance and resentment. Usually, when tithing advocates see that they cannot impose Malachi 3 on believers under the New Testament, they resort to saying that tithing predated the Law. There is a serious problem of inconsistency with that line of argument. There are many things that predated the law, polygamy, circumcision, burnt offerings, building altars and other things but these things are not part of the New Testament way of doing things. But we teach that giving should be done out of love, whether it follows an Old Testament pattern or a New Testament pattern. There is no reason for preachers to threaten anyone or curse anyone since Jesus came to set people who were under the law free from the curse of the law and everyone free from sin and death.

The burnt offering (Leviticus 1; 6:8–13; 8:18-21; 16:24)

The first reference to "burnt offerings" is Genesis 8:20. “Then Noah built an altar to the LORD and took some of every clean animal and some of every clean bird and offered burnt offerings on the altar. And when the LORD smelled the pleasing aroma, the LORD said in his heart, "I will never again curse the ground because of man, for the intention of man's heart is evil from his youth. Neither will I ever again strike down every living creature as I have done. While the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night, shall not cease." (Gen 8:20-22 ESV)  In Exodus 10:25, where Moses explained to Pharaoh, we read, "But Moses said, "You must also let us have sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God."

The burnt offering was a voluntary act of worship to express devotion or commitment to God. It was also used as an atonement for unintentional sin. The elements of the burnt offering were a bull, a bird, or a ram without blemish. The common Israelite worshipper brought a male animal (a bull, lamb, goat, pigeon, or turtledove depending on the wealth of the worshipper) to the door of the tabernacle. The meat and bones and organs of the animal were to be totally burnt, and this was God’s portion. The burnt offering was a sacrifice that was completely burnt. None of it was to be eaten at all, and therefore the fire consumed the whole sacrifice.

“If you offer one of your cattle as a burnt offering, it must be a bull that has nothing wrong with it. You must take the animal to the entrance of the Meeting Tent where the LORD will accept the offering.” (Lev 1:3 ERV)

The animal had to be without blemish. The worshipper then placed his hands upon the head of the animal and in awareness that this innocent animal was standing in for the sinner he would seek the Lord for forgiveness and then killed the animal immediately.

“Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When any one of you brings an offering to the LORD, you shall bring your offering of livestock from the herd or from the flock. If his offering is a burnt offering from the herd, he shall offer a male without blemish. He shall bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting, that he may be accepted before the LORD. He shall lay his hand on the head of the burnt offering, and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him. Then he shall kill the bull before the LORD, and Aaron's sons the priests shall bring the blood and throw the blood against the sides of the altar that is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.” (Lev 1:2-5 ESV) 
The priests were also responsible to wash various parts of the animal before putting it on the altar: “Then he shall flay the burnt offering and cut it into pieces, and the sons of Aaron the priest shall put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire. And Aaron's sons the priests shall arrange the pieces, the head, and the fat, on the wood that is on the fire on the altar; but its entrails and its legs he shall wash with water. And the priest shall burn all of it on the altar, as a burnt offering, a food offering with a pleasing aroma to the LORD.” (Lev 1:6-9 ESV)

Later In Israel's history there were burnt offerings made twice per day, one at morning and one at evening: “The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, "Command the people of Israel and say to them, 'My offering, my food for my food offerings, my pleasing aroma, you shall be careful to offer to me at its appointed time.' And you shall say to them, This is the food offering that you shall offer to the LORD: two male lambs a year old without blemish, day by day, as a regular offering. also a tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a grain offering, mixed with a quarter of a hin of beaten oil. It is a regular burnt offering, which was ordained at Mount Sinai for a pleasing aroma, a food offering to the LORD.” (Num 28:1-6 ESV)

The Burnt offering was performed to atone for the peoples sins against the Lord and was a dedication offering of one’s life before the Lord continually. The animal’s hide was given to the Levites, who could later sell it to earn money for themselves.

The grain offering and drink offering (Leviticus 2; 6:14–23)

The Israelites offered meal (cereals) or vegetables in addition to the animals. The Hebrew term for "grain offering" is minha, which, means generally "gift, present, tribute." The fruit of the field was offered in the form of a cake or baked bread made of grain, fine flour with oil and salt. All meal offerings were made with oil and salt and no honey and leaven were to be used (oil and salt preserved while honey and leaven would spoil). The meal offerings were brought to one of the priests, who took it to the altar and cast a "memorial portion" on the fire and he did this also with the incense. The priest was to offer a part of the grain offering on the burnt offering altar as a "memorial (portion)" to the Lord along with the salt of the covenant.

"When you give a grain offering to the LORD, your offering must be made from fine flour. You must pour oil on this flour and put frankincense on it. Then you must bring it to Aaron's sons, the priests. One of them will take a handful of the fine flour with oil and frankincense in it. He will bring this part, which represents the whole grain offering, to the altar. There it will be burned up as a sweet-smelling gift to the LORD. The rest of that grain offering will belong to Aaron and his sons. This gift to the LORD is very holy.” (Lev 2:1-3 ERV) 
“Also, you must put salt on every grain offering you bring. You must not forget to add salt, because it represents God's agreement with you. Always put salt on these offerings.” (Lev 2:13 ERV) 

The preserving character of salt suggests the enduring nature of the covenant bond between the Lord and his people. The sinner could offer dough from wheat flour baked in an oven, cooked on a griddle, fried in a pan, or roasted to make bread.

"If you give a grain offering that was baked in the oven, it must be unleavened bread made from fine flour mixed with oil, or it must be wafers with oil poured over them. If you bring a grain offering cooked in a baking pan, it must be made from fine flour without yeast and mixed with oil. You must break it into pieces and pour oil over it. It is a grain offering. If you bring a grain offering cooked in a frying pan, it must be made from fine flour mixed with oil. "When you bring grain offerings made from these things to the LORD, you must give them to the priest, and he will take them to the altar. Then the priest will take part of the grain offering and lift it up as a memorial offering. He will bring it to the altar to be burned up as a sweet-smelling gift to the LORD.” (Lev 2:4-9 ERV) 
"You must not give any grain offering to the LORD that has yeast in it. You must not burn yeast or honey as a gift to the LORD. You may bring yeast and honey to the LORD as an offering from the first harvest, but they must not be put on the altar to be burned as a sweet smell.” (Lev 2:11-12 ERV)  First harvest (first fruits) of yeast and honey could be offered unto the Lord but not as a burnt offering.
"If you bring a grain offering from the first harvest to the LORD, you must bring roasted heads of grain. They must be crushed heads of fresh grain. This will be your grain offering from the first harvest. You must put oil and frankincense on it. It is a grain offering. The priest must burn part of the crushed grain, the oil, and all the frankincense on it as a memorial offering. It is a gift to the LORD.” (Lev 2:14-16 ERV) 
The priests were given a portion of this offering, but it had to be eaten within the court of the tabernacle. “The rest of that grain offering will belong to Aaron and his sons. This gift to the LORD is very holy.” (Lev 2:10 ERV) "This is the law for the grain offering: The sons of Aaron will bring it to the front of the altar as an offering to the LORD. There must be some oil and frankincense on the grain offering. The priest will take a handful of fine flour from the grain offering and burn it on the altar as a sweet-smelling memorial offering to the LORD. "Aaron and his sons will use the rest of that grain to make bread without yeast. This must be eaten in a holy place--in the courtyard around the Meeting Tent. I have given this part of the grain offering as the priests' share of the gifts offered to me. Like the sin offering and the guilt offering, it is most holy. It must not be baked with yeast. Any male descendant of Aaron may eat from these gifts to the LORD. This is their share forever throughout your generations. Whatever touches these offerings will be made holy." The LORD said to Moses, "This is the offering that Aaron and his sons must bring to the LORD when Aaron is anointed to be the high priest. They must bring 8 cups of fine flour for a grain offering. This will be offered at the times of the daily offering--half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening. The fine flour must be mixed with oil and baked on a pan. After it is cooked, you must bring it in, break it into pieces, and offer it as a sweet-smelling gift to LORD. "In the future, when Aaron's descendants take their place as the anointed priests, they will continue to make this grain offering to the Lord. This rule will continue forever. &#x54;he grain offering must be completely burned for the LORD. Every grain offering that a priest gives must be completely burned. It must not be eaten." (Lev 6:14-23 ERV) For as long as the priesthood continued, this offering had to continue.
"At the time someone brings their offering, they must also give a grain offering to the LORD. The grain offering will be 8 cups of fine flour mixed with 1 quart of olive oil. Each time you offer a lamb as a burnt offering or sacrifice, you must also prepare a quart of wine as a drink offering.  "If you are giving a ram, you must also prepare a grain offering. This grain offering should be 16 cups of fine flour mixed with 1 1/4 quarts of olive oil. And you must prepare 1 1/4 quarts of wine as a drink offering. Its smell will please the LORD.” (Num 15:4-7 ERV) 

Take note of these words: “I have given this part of the grain offering as the priests' share of the gifts offered to me.” God also spoke in the same manner concerning tithes. It is important for a Bible reader to notice the weights God gives to certain issues. Tithes and offerings were listed under ordinances and they had certain rules to be observed when they were offered. Well, I have done much in presenting this information.
