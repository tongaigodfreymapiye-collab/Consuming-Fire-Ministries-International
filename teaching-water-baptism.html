What Is Christian Water Baptism?

By Apostle T. G. Mapiye

In the early days of Christianity, altar calls were not made by the raising of hands or the calling forward to the front. These are methods which have been adopted by the modern church to help a person act out his decision. People would indicate their decision to follow Christ by being baptized following repentance and confession of sins. It is almost similar to the baptism of John. It involves immersing the person being baptized into water once and coming up out from the water.

Why be baptized? Is Baptism necessary for Salvation?

Baptism in water is purely a symbol and it does not save us. Some misguided Christians think that water baptism is necessary for salvation. Baptism is a testimony of our faith and a public declaration that we believe in Jesus Christ. The Scriptures tell us that we have eternal life the moment we believe (John 5:24), and belief always comes before being baptized. Those who believe that baptism is required for salvation are quick to use 1 Peter 3:21 as a “proof text,” because it states “baptism now saves you.” Was Peter really saying that the act of being baptized is what saves us? If he were, he would be contradicting many other passages of Scripture that clearly show people being saved (as evidenced by their receiving the Holy Spirit) prior to being baptized or without being baptized at all (like the thief on the cross in Luke 23:39-43). A good example of someone who was saved before being baptized is Cornelius and his household in Acts 10. The Bible is clear that salvation is by grace through faith in Jesus Christ, not by works of any kind, including baptism (Ephesians 2:8-9). Repentance is necessary for the forgiveness of sins but repentance alone cannot save, one must believe in the Lord Jesus Christ as Lord and Saviour in order to be saved. Throughout the Bible we see that at the point of faith a believer possesses all the promises and blessings of salvation (John 1:12; 3:16; 5:24; 6:47; 20:31; Acts 10:43; 13:39; 16:31). When one believes, he has eternal life, does not come under judgment, and has passed from death into life (John 5:24)—all before he or she is baptized. If baptism were required for salvation, then no one could be saved without another party being present. Someone must be there to baptize a person before he can be saved. This effectively limits who can be saved and when he can be saved.

“He who believes and is baptized will be saved, but he who does not believe will be condemned.” (Mark 16:16)

Nowhere in the Bible do we find a statement such as “whoever is not baptized will be condemned.” Therefore, we cannot say that baptism is necessary for salvation based on Mark 16:16 or any other verse. Jesus did not say that those who are not baptized shall be damned but He only said that those who do not believe shall be damned. The one specific condition required for salvation is stated in the second part of Mark 16:16: “Whoever does not believe will be condemned.” In essence, Jesus has given both the positive condition of belief (whoever believes will be saved) and the negative condition of unbelief (whoever does not believe will be condemned). It is also necessary for one to believe before being baptized. How can someone be baptized if they do not believe? Baptism in water is for believers and not unbelievers. It is an outward expression of an inward experience. Without the inward experience, the outward expression would not make sense. If a person is not born again and gets baptized in water, he will still be a sinner - a wet sinner. Peter writes in his epistle saying that water baptism does not remove the filth of the flesh but is the answer of a good conscience toward God (1 Pet. 3:20, 21). We are not saved by outward ordinances but rather by our faith in the Lord Jesus Christ.

To be a true disciple we must be baptized. Baptism fulfils all righteousness.

“Then Jesus came from Galilee to Jordan, to John, to be baptized by him. But John restrained Him, saying, I have need to be baptized by You, and do You come to me? And answering Jesus said to him, Allow it now, for it is becoming to us to fulfill all righteousness. Then he allowed Him. And Jesus, when He had been baptized, went up immediately out of the water….” (Mat 3:13-16)

Memory Verse: Luke 9:23-24 “Then he said to them all: If anyone would come after me, he must deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me. For whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for me will save it.”

To identify ourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ. Baptism is done to bury the old self, or to crucify the sinful nature. It has a spiritual effect. Knowing that you have actually buried the sinful nature makes you realize that you are now walking in the newness of life. Baptism joins us together in the likeness of Jesus’ death.

“Do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into His death? Therefore we were buried with Him by baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father; even so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we have been joined together in the likeness of His death, we shall also be in the likeness of His resurrection; knowing this, that our old man is crucified with Him in order that the body of sin might be destroyed, that from now on we should not serve sin.” (Rom 6:3-6)
“I have been crucified with Christ, and I live; yet no longer I, but Christ lives in me. And that life that I now live in the flesh, I live by faith toward the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself on my behalf.” (Gal 2:20).

Who can be baptized?

Children cannot be baptized until they reach an age of maturity to make a decision for Christ. Baptism is for both men and women.

“But when they believed Philip preaching the gospel, the things concerning the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women.” (Acts 8:12)

Baptism is for people who have believed the preaching of the gospel, the things concerning the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ. Any man or woman who has decided to become a true disciple of Jesus Christ can be baptized.

“Then Peter said to them, Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ to remission of sins, and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all those afar off, as many as the Lord our God shall call.” (Acts 2:38-39).

Repentance is for repentant people. A person whose heart has not resolved to quit sin cannot be baptized. John the Baptist rebuked certain unrepentant people for coming to be baptized.

“But seeing many of the Pharisees and Sadducees come to his baptism, he said to them, O generation of vipers, who has warned you to flee from the wrath to come? Bring forth therefore fruits worthy of repentance.” (Mat 3:7-8)

A person showing forth fruits worthy of repentance can be baptized with water. A disciple of Christ or someone whose decision is to become a true disciple of Christ should be baptized.

Correct Method of Baptism

The word ‘baptize’ literally means ‘immerse’ in the Greek New Testament. The root word for ‘baptize’ - ‘baptizo.’ ‘Baptizo’ literally means to dip, to sink, plunge, immerse, submerge and cover wholly with the element used in baptism. John the Baptist chose a place with much water for baptizing (Jn. 3:23). If water baptism had been by any other mode but immersion there would not have been the necessity for much water. The New Testament actually uses the two phrases ‘up’ and ‘out of’ together indicating that Jesus had been immersed. The Ethiopian eunuch had to go ‘down into’ the water and ‘up out of’ the water (Acts 8:38, 39).

Who can baptize another?

A mature believer who is filled with the Holy Spirit and knows the correct way of Baptism can baptize.

Christian water baptism is done in the name of the Lord Jesus. In water baptism, the minister (the baptizer) takes us (the subject) and places us into water (the medium). Holy Spirit Baptism can be received through the laying on of hands by a Holy Spirit filled believer. In the baptism in the Holy Spirit, Jesus (the baptizer) takes us (the subject) and places us into the Holy Spirit (the medium). The Holy Spirit baptism can have an evidence of people speaking in tongues, giving prophecies, seeing visions and other supernatural manifestations of the Holy Spirit.

Where and how to be Baptized?

In a place where there is some water

Both the Baptizer and the person to be baptized should go down into the water once and come up out of the water in the name of Jesus Christ. The water must be water enough to cover the body of the person being baptized. The person being baptized must be willing to be baptized. A person can be baptized anywhere where there is much water even inside a swimming pool, water basin, Jacuzzi, river etc.

“And as they passed along the way, they came on some water. And the eunuch said, See, here is water, what hinders me from being baptized? Philip said, If you believe with all your heart, it is lawful. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God. And he commanded the chariot to stand still. And they both went down into the water, both Philip and the eunuch. And he baptized him. And when they had come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught Philip away, so that the eunuch saw him no more. And he went on his way rejoicing.” (Acts 8:36-39)

This is the correct way to baptize. Sprinkling and infant water baptism or drawing crosses on the forehead are wrong methods of baptism. Jesus Himself was not baptized as an infant but when He was about to begin public ministry. Baptism is not sprinkling water because the water must be enough to cover the human body.

“And Jesus, when He had been baptized, went up immediately out of the water….” (Mt 3:16)

Finally, water baptism is to show forth our death, burial and resurrection in Christ Jesus (Rom. 6:4, 5). What better way is there than immersion to show forth all three aspects of the work of Christ in our lives? When a person dies, we don’t just sprinkle them with sand. We bury them.

In the name of Jesus Christ

In the New Testament, we see that Jesus gave the command for water baptism as ‘baptizing them in the Name of the Father, and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit’ (Matt. 28:19). In the book of Acts, we see the disciples baptizing in the Name of the Lord Jesus Christ (Acts 2:38; 8:16; 10:48;19:5). Amen.
