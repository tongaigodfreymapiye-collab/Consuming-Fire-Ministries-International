Guidelines for Preaching

By Apostle T. G. Mapiye

Guidelines for Preaching

‘And He said to them, "Go into all the world and preach the gospel to all creation.’ (Mark 16:15)

"The Word of God can grow to be only a hunting-ground for texts; and we can preach, meaning intensely every word we utter, and yet in reality only lost for the moment like an actor in his part, or at least leaving it to the folk to live it out; for us, bless me, we have no time for that, but are already immersed, poor harried souls, in determining what we shall preach on next."- A. J. Gossip

“Give me one hundred preachers who fear nothing but sin and desire nothing but God, and I care not whether they be clergymen or laymen, they alone will shake the gates of Hell and set up the kingdom of Heaven upon Earth.” ~ John Wesley

“If Jesus preached the same message minister’s preach today, He would have never been crucified.” ~ Leonard Ravenhill

“The gospel we preach must not be just something we hear from men or read from books or even conceived through our meditation. Unless it is delivered to us by God, it can serve no spiritual utility.” ~ Watchman Nee

“It is no use walking anywhere to preach unless our walking is our preaching.” ~ Francis of Assisi

“I’d rather be able to pray than to be a great preacher; Jesus Christ never taught his disciples how to preach, but only how to pray.” ~ D.L. Moody

“We complain today that ministers do not know how to preach; but is it not equally true that our congregations do not know how to hear?” ~ J.I. Packer

“What is the chief end of preaching? I like to think it is this: It is to give men and women a sense of God and His presence.” ~ Martyn Lloyd-Jones

Definition

In simple terms, to preach is to publicly proclaim or teach (a religious message or belief). The word preaching in Hebrew has three words, ‘basar’, which means "to bring or tell good tidings" (Psalms 40:9; Isaiah 61:1), ‘qara', which means "to call," or "proclaim" (Nehemiah 6:7; Jonah 3:2), and ‘qeri'ah’, which means "cry," or "preaching" (Jonah 3:2); and in the Greek, ‘kerux’, which means "crier," or "herald" (1 Timothy 2:7), or ‘kerusso’, which means "to cry or proclaim as a herald" (Matthew 3:1; Romans 10:14), and ‘euaggellizo’, which means "to announce good news" (Matthew 11:5)).

Paul instructed Timothy, ‘When Christ Jesus comes as king, he will be the judge of everyone, whether they are living or dead. So with God and Christ as witnesses, I command you to preach God's message. Do it willingly, even if it isn't the popular thing to do. You must correct people and point out their sins. But also cheer them up, and when you instruct them, always be patient. The time is coming when people won't listen to good teaching. Instead, they will look for teachers who will please them by telling them only what they are itching to hear. They will turn from the truth and eagerly listen to senseless stories.’ (2 Timothy 4:1-4 CEV)

It is not always the case that when a preacher preaches God’s message people will respond positively. Sometimes people don’t want God’s message to be preached to them, instead they want the preacher to preach sermons that will tickle them and make them feel comfortable in their wrong ways. A preacher of God’s word should not bend to follow the desires of people but should always have the message that God wants to be preached to His people.

Preparation of the Vessel

‘How then will they call on Him in whom they have not believed? How will they believe in Him whom they have not heard? And how will they hear without a preacher? How will they preach unless they are sent? Just as it is written, "HOW BEAUTIFUL ARE THE FEET OF THOSE WHO BRING GOOD NEWS OF GOOD THINGS!" (Romans 10:14-15)

The preacher is not the source, but is a channel that God uses to convey his messages. The life of the preacher must radiate the reality of what he preaches. Before you appear before people to preach God’s message it is important to spend time alone with God and His Word. Prayerfully seek out the counsel, wisdom and instructions of the Lord in through studying the Word. Due to the law of fast forgetting it is important for the preacher to keep a notebook in which to record the thoughts and lessons that are taught by the Holy Spirit during Bible study times.

Preparation of the Message

”The preacher is not a chef; he’s a waiter. God doesn’t want you to make the meal; He just wants you to deliver it to the table without messing it up. That’s all.” ~ John MacArthur

A Good sermon shout have the following:

1) Text

2) Central truth

3) Introduction

4) Body

5) Conclusion

Tell them what you are going to say (the introduction), then say it (the body) and then tell them what you said (the conclusion). Do not bore people by preaching your whole sermon again, just highlight your main points. This will refresh their memory of something you may have said thirty minutes ago.

A preacher should be able to:

1) Choose an appropriate text for a biblical message.

2) Identify God's Good News in a text.

3) Cultivate attitudes and actions of gratitude, trust and obedience in the lives of listeners.

4) Show people how to respond to the sermon he/she has preached.

5) Set a goal for each sermon. Planning and setting goals for preaching in very vital. Not only is planning important to your overall preaching ministry, it is also imperative to the preparation and delivery of each message you preach.

6) Evaluate your sermons to see if you have met your goal, and discern whether or not your preaching ministry provides a balanced spiritual diet that can help the ones to whom you minister grow in the faith.

7) Live out the sermons he/she preaches if he/she finds himself/herself under similar circumstances.

8) Show people the central truth in the sermon. Every message you preach should have a central truth taken from the Scripture text upon which the sermon is based.

9) Give brief, simple instructions. Peter told the crowd exactly what they were supposed to do to respond to his message (Acts 2:38–39)

Your sermon should be able to achieve the following:

a) gaining the attention of the hearers.

b) explaining, illustrating, and applying the content of the Scripture passage.

c) guaranteeing spiritual response.

d) pointing the hearers to a specific course of action as a result of the message.

To come up with a relevant sermon:

Know your audience - Jesus knew the needs common to all people (Matthew 6:31–32) as well as the needs of each person (John 1:43–50) and this made him able to minister relevant sermons for people. The Holy Spirit will guide you as you seek to minister to the needs of the people. Trust and follow His leadership (1 Corinthians 2:10–12). Expect people to respond to your sermon and to achieve your goal at the end of your sermon. There is a story I once read that of a preacher who approached the distinguished preacher C. H. Spurgeon and asked, “How do we preach the same gospel and you have results and I don’t?” Spurgeon answered, “You don’t expect results every time you preach, do you?” “No,” replied the preacher. “Then that is one reason why you don’t get any!” Spurgeon exclaimed. When you preach, expect God to make something happen.

The goal of the sermon helps us to answer which one of the following questions:

a) What do I want this message to accomplish?

b) What does the text say?

c) How do I apply these truths to my hearers?

Things to avoid when preaching:

The preacher becomes the point of the sermon - This kind of preaching exalts the man and loses the gospel message.

The preacher begins to entertain the audience at the expense of the truth - While it is important to engage the audience the preacher should not get carried away and begin to entertain the audience instead of giving them God’s message.

Intellectual pride - No matter how intelligent you are and how much research you have done, be careful not to make your vocabulary, knowledge and presentation skills become the focus of your congregation after ministering to them.

Preaching at random without any goals - Where there is no sermon goal, the one who ministers generally tends to drift from subject to subject without developing any course of action for the hearers to take.

Being boring to the listeners - Your sermon should not be confused or arranged disorderly such that people spend more than 30 minutes trying to understand what you are preaching about. The preacher should be engaging and not boring because the text is alive with the power of God. Jesus told countless parables (Mark 4:34). He pulled spiritual truth from everyday life. Use illustrations, examples and memorable stories to convey your message.

Looking for verses to support your personal agenda and ideas - There is a way to preach the Bible unBiblically to support a personal agenda. Such preaching should be avoided. Just because you have quoted a Bible verse does not make your sermon Biblical.

Imposing your own meaning to the text - For example, the apostle Paul’s assurance that not even a hair of your heads will be lost (Acts 27:34) is not intended as a solution for baldness. The plain, literal meaning of a passage should be used unless it cannot be taken literally. Therefore, if you will take a text from Scriptures and keep close to it, you will avoid any twisting of the Scriptures.

Using only one Bible translation - avoid preaching a sermon based

on one translation of a certain text. Let your selected Bible passage

be clear and confirmed by multiple translations.

Trying to convert people by your own power and wisdom – You should never think that you can convert souls using your own power of by tricking them and lying to them.

Being too long and talkative - Some who minister have a tendency to talk unnecessarily about things that do not concern the overall objective of the sermon. When you preach, everything you say must contribute to meeting that same goal. Be mindful of the goal of the message. Your message objective is part of your ministry objective: to declare the whole purpose of God (Acts 20:27) so that the lost will find Christ and the believers will mature in the faith.

“The gospel is preached in the ears of all men; it only comes with power to some. The power that is in the gospel does not lie in the eloquence of the preacher otherwise men would be converters of souls. Nor does it lie in the preacher’s learning; otherwise it could consists of the wisdom of men. We might preach till our tongues rotted, till we should exhaust our lungs and die, but never a soul would be converted unless there were mysterious power going with it – the Holy Ghost changing the will of man. O Sirs! We might as well preach to stone walls as preach to humanity unless the Holy Ghost be with the word, to give it power to convert the soul.” ― Charles Haddon Spurgeon

Preaching Methods and Sermon Types:

Preaching through a Book

Preach a series of sermons through a biblical book. Instead of struggling to know what to preach about every week, preach through a book. As you preach through a book, the Spirit of God will take the Word of God and apply to the needs of the people of God. Preaching a series of sermons will also enable you preach on many topics and not just address your favourite topics. If you preach through a book, reading verse by verse and chapter by chapter, the congregation can benefit more out of your sermons. Avoid preaching the same or similar texts, subjects, and messages. Like Paul, preach the whole purpose of God (Acts 20:26–27). Study your preaching patterns carefully to see that you are not neglecting the great truths and texts of Scripture.

Bible passage based sermon

Passage-based sermons start with a verse, a group of verses, a chapter, or a book and analyse that passage in a particular way to develop a message to meet the needs of the audience.

Bible subject based sermon

Subject-based sermons, on the other hand, begin with a topic rather than a passage and focus the teachings of the Scripture from various passages on the one subject.

Preaching without notes

I have practiced this kind of preaching, where the message is spontaneous, and usually presented without notes at the time of delivery. This method is very effective when one is led by the Holy Spirit.

Pulpit Etiquette

Dress decently, modestly, smartly to look presentable and appropriately for before standing on the pulpit to preach.

Colossians 4:6, “Let your speech be alway with grace,seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”

Luke 4:22, “And all bare him witness, and wondered at the gracious words which proceeded out of his mouth...”

When preaching, do not look at the same person in the room. Look around.

Make eye contact with all the people and do not be shy.

Do make appropriate gestures to help illustrate the message.

Be loud as to be understood by all. Preach with emotion and charisma—do not be a boring preacher!

Do not be in a bad mood. You are not fit to be a preacher if you cannot rule your emotions! (Proverbs 25:28, 16:32).

Enjoy your sermon.
