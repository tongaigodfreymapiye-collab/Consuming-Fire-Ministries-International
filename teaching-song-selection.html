Guidelines for Selection of Songs for Church Meetings

By Apostle T. G. Mapiye

Guidelines for Selection of Songs for Church Meetings

The Process of Selecting Songs for Church Service Praise and Worship

Being part of the Church Music Team is a wonderful privilege. It is important to allow God to use you as a team and guide you to select songs that will be used to glorify Him. You should have an understanding of the lyrics you sing and the ability to convey that understanding, love for the people you lead, ask God for wisdom in selecting the songs and verses used for worship. Selecting songs and singing them is not the only thing you should do but you also need the ability to act on the truths you sing and speak. Ask God to give you the humility to lead in a way that glorifies God instead of yourself or the congregation and the ability to guide the congregation to an improved relationship with God.

Take note of the following important guidelines:

1) Pray and seek God’s guidance

There are many good songs that people can sing and many preferences. When we lead in worship we need to understand the congregation we are leading and what God is doing with the congregation. Spend time waiting on the Lord a few days before the practice session, so that God can guide you in songs selection and also prepare people's hearts for what He wants to communicate. We have a tendency to want to sing our favourite songs and this desire can divert our attention from listening to what the Holy Spirit is saying to the Church.

2) Understand Your Pastor

Know exactly what the Pastor wants and where the pastor is taking the Church. Music should not go in opposite direction with the direction of the pastor. The pastor knows what the Church needs at any given season. There are songs which are nice and enjoyable at personal level but may not be the best songs for the Church in certain seasons. Your focus should not be on what you enjoy but on what is good for the Church. Take seriously the songs suggested by the Pastor to be sung and put them on your list.

3) Choose songs that are biblically accurate

Scripture says to “worship in spirit and in truth” (John 4:23). It is easier to worship and praise a God that you know and have a relationship with than to know songs and lead people to worship when you lack a direct relationship with the God you are leading others to worship. It is important to know the God you worship, therefore immerse yourself in the scripture that reveals Him. This doesn’t mean that every song has to quote the written word of God, but the content should square with what the Bible says about Him. Select songs which do not go against the doctrine and teachings of your Pastor. Be sure that the music you select rightly represents the truth of who God is, not the mere image of who He is thought to be.

4) Select songs which are relevant.

Don’t be afraid to teach a new song that really expresses what God is doing in the greater body of Christ –one with a fresh perspective, but don’t overwhelm them by teaching too many songs at one time. When you introduce a new song, give people printed lyrics of the song on paper so that they can sing along. When you introduce a song in another language which is not Shona or English, please translate the lyrics so that people will know that they are singing. One new song at a time is a good rule of thumb.

5) When the Pastor changes your selection of songs

Pastors have the duty to feed the flock and lead the whole Church. Pastors know the needs of the Church and prepare for the service. Sometimes they will guide you in the choice of songs to communicate a certain message to the Church.

6) Choose God-centered songs over “Me” songs or Self-centred songs

There are songs that say we are going to do this or that, and that make declarations and claims God’s promises but we are primarily there to worship God in the person of Jesus Christ. Let’s celebrate who He is, what He has done

and what He is still doing through His Holy Spirit in the name of Jesus Christ. We should sing songs that call attention to His glory (not ours). There are times when we need to make declarations and encourage ourselves in the Lord through music but most of the time we must sing praise and worship songs.

7) Support the Theme and Season

What has God been speaking to you and to the church? Is it a time of joy and celebration or mourning or somewhere in between? It’s always a good idea to ask your Pastor about the theme or to observe the sermons being preached. Pastors are always in tune with what the church is going through. Does the church need: hope, joy, healing, repentance, reconciliation, or understanding? Support the theme of the Church, but don’t be bound by it to forget the voice of God. So your personal theme and your favourite songs should not overshadow the theme of the Church and the voice of the Holy Spirit.

8) Do not complicate your list of songs for the service

The message of the songs must be clear and appropriate without creating monotony. If your theme is about the Power of God or the Holy Spirit, then make a list with at least 80% of the songs carrying different messages about the theme. Don't just mix songs haphazardly without order. Arrange your songs in order, preferably ascending order, starting low and going high until people reach the peak in praise and in worship.

9) Choose songs that encourage participation

Enjoy the Lord in front of them and they will want to get involved. Make it easy to do so by choosing songs that makes people participants and not spectators. Instead of, "I worship You,", let your songs have words like, "We worship You," to cater for the congregation. They should feel involved in the song.

10) Know the nature of Service and environment that you will sing under

The environment will have an impact on the songs you select. Will the music you are considering perform well in a small group, or is it best to be delivered to a larger audience? Can it be led by one person or will it be led by a team? If you have visitors coming, know that the first impression of your performance that they will get will last long in their minds.
