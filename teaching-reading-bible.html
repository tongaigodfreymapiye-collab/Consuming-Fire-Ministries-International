Guidelines for Reading the Bible

By Apostle T. G. Mapiye

How to read the Bible

“Be diligent to present yourself approved to God as a workman who does not need to be ashamed, handling accurately the word of truth.” 2 Timothy 2:15

After Jesus rose from the dead, one of the things He did was to explain the Scriptures to his disciples. In Luke chapter twenty four, verse twenty seven, we read, “And beginning with Moses and with all the prophets, He explained to them the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures.” Even today, Jesus through the Holy Spirit is explaining the things concerning Himself through the Scriptures. Make a commitment to hear the Lord’s voice through the pages of your Bible.

It is not healthy for Christians to live without spending time in prayers and in reading their Bibles.

Many people browse through pages of the Bible without careful study and in most cases miss the main message that the Scriptures convey. It is very possible to read the Bible and understand it.

The Bible is inspired, and we must seek out its complete message in order to provide a Scriptural balance to our interpretation. When we read the Bible, our aim should be to extract the meaning of the text and not to read into the text our own beliefs or ideas. Avoid self-centered reading of the Bible supposing that any or all parts apply to you or your group in a way that they do not apply to everyone else. It is an error to read a single verse in isolation, forgetting that it has an immediate context and the original context and cultural setting of the Bible. It is very common for people to read into a biblical narrative suggestions or ideas that come from contemporary culture that are simultaneously foreign to the narrator’s purpose and contradictory to his point of view. They ignore the Holy Spirit and appropriate the text for purposes that are quite foreign to the biblical narrative. Another serious problem is changing the original definitions of certain words in the Bible to suit a church doctrine. When the plain meaning of the text leaves people cold, producing no immediate spiritual delight or saying something other than what they wish it said, they are often tempted to redefine it to mean something else. Sometimes people do this out of ignorance and sometimes deliberately to benefit something through such misinterpretations. Having said this, the Holy Spirit is our teacher and guide into all truth and must always be obeyed.

Scripture often interprets itself. The Bible is its own best interpreter. A good example is the parable of the sower in Luke chapter 8. After Jesus shares the parable, his disciples asked him what this parable meant? Jesus answered them and gave them the meaning of the parable. If someone comes up with a different meaning from what the Lord Jesus clearly taught, then we know that the person has misinterpreted the Bible passage.

The words although written in ink and in human languages came from God. The inspiration came from God even though human authors were employed to put the words in written form. It is important to ask God for help through the Holy Spirit, every time we want to read the Bible. The way a person interprets the Bible will affect their walk with Christ and the beliefs they hold. You are responsible for your own interpretation of the Bible. One common mistake that many people make when reading the Bible is to let the issues of our day interpret the Bible. God, through the Holy Spirit inspired the human writers of Scripture, who used their own languages in their historical setting to convey their meaning. The author's meaning should control our interpretation of any passage of Scripture and we should never impose our own meanings to the Scriptures. When we do this we are Biblical and sound.

Before we interpret the Bible we must establish the original author’s intention both in time and logic in any book we choose to read.

Words have meaning only in context; clauses have meaning only in context; sentences have meaning only in context. Do not rush to apply verses of Scripture without understanding the whole passage or paragraph. Interpretation and understanding must happen before application.

In applying the Bible, it is important to remember that (except in prophecy) one and only one meaning is valid for a particular Bible text. Many possible applications may be derived from this one meaning.

The Bible must be seen as God’s written word before we read it. To read the Bible thoughtlessly or carelessly or academically or professionally is not to read the Bible as God’s written Word. Our attitude and approach towards the Bible can affect our understanding.

How to make Bible Study Notes

Before you read any book of the Bible, you need to understand the following background:

  1. The author. Who wrote the book?
  2. The date. When was the written?
  3. The recipients. Who were the original recipients of the book? The most important step in interpreting any biblical text is to first work very hard to discover what the original author was trying to communicate to the original audience.
  4. The specific reason for writing. What are the main reasons the author wrote the book?
  5. Aspects of the cultural setting that relate to the purpose of the writing. What was the cultural context in the time the book was written and received?
  6. References to historical people and events. Who are the people and what was the significance of certain events mentioned by the author?
  7. Type of literature and the nature of the book you are reading. Is it Poetry? Is it a parable? Is it an Epistle? Is it a Law? Is it an allegory? Or is it a narrative? Is it a Proverb? Or is it a History book? The type of literature used can help in understanding the message being conveyed.
  8. The context. The context of a verse exists at various levels - textual, literary and historical. The first is its immediate textual context. A word is found in a sentence, a sentence in a paragraph and a paragraph in a chapter.

When you do a personal Bible study, you can get more insight and understand the true meanings of certain Bible passages. As a Christian, you may come across people who will disagree with certain interpretations of some portions of the Bible. When this happens, you need to handle disagreements of interpretation with the love Christ. Sometimes you could be wrong and need to re-read the Bible on that particular area and sometimes you could be right. When you find yourself in confusion, seek for the help of your pastor, and if you are in doubt, pray to God for clarity and do personal research on the matter asking God to show you the truth.

Lastly, I want to say to you, do not rush to put a full stop when you learn about something in the Bible, put a comma, because as you grow in the Lord, more light is shed by the Holy Spirit. There is need to seek for the whole counsel of God on every matter. There are no new gospels, no new revelations that can be greater than what Jesus has already revealed in Scriptures. If any man should come to you and teach you what is contrary to the teachings of the Bible, it is better to hold on to the truth you know to be true in the Bible that to make a gamble with your faith.
