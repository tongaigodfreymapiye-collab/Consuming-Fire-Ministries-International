THE KINGDOM OF GOD 1

By Apostle T. G. Mapiye

“Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.” (Matthew 6:10)

I greet all of you in the name of Jesus Christ. We are going to embark on a new series. Our thrust this year is the greatness of God. God placed it upon my heart to share with you all that we must experience the life of Christ to the full and we must manifest the greatness of God.

“For the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking but of righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.” (Romans 14:17 ESV)
“Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom.” (Luke 12:32 ESV)

It is the will of God that we live victoriously and successfully here on earth. God did not call us to punish us with sicknesses, poverty and death. We can serve God better when we have more than enough and when we are healthy and alive. When we need to spend, we would spend wisely; when we need to eat, we would eat properly. It is God’s will for His children to find jobs after graduation from college, to get paid when salary is due, to have school fees when it due and to be able to manifest God’s love towards other people.

I have been a Christian for over 20 years and I have discovered that teaching the word of God is very good and it can help people to change some of their habits and bad behavior but no matter how much people are taught, teaching alone does not make one to become a child of God. I have seen people training their dogs to act like human beings, but no amount of training can turn a dog into a human being. The dog cannot become a human unless it is born as a human. A dog cannot enter into the human kingdom through education. In order to enter into the human kingdom, a dog has to possess the human life. This is why going to Church for all this while will not be able to truly change the hearts of some of you! We entered the human kingdom by being born through our parents.

“That which is born of the flesh is flesh and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.” (John 3:6 KJV)

The need to be born again

“But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God, who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God.” (John 1:12–13 ESV)
“They were made God’s children not because of genetics or biological descent, or because of human procreation, but because of the transformation of mind, heart and character brought about by God.” (John 1:13 Remedy)

The Kingdom of God is for people who have been transformed in their mind, heart and character by God!

You cannot say that you were transformed when there is no change in your way of thinking about sin, about God and about the Kingdom of God. Untransformed people will always resist the teachings of the Bible.

“I tell you this, brothers: flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor does the perishable inherit the imperishable.” (1 Corinthians 15:50 ESV)

Jesus said, “Very truly I tell you, no one can see the kingdom of God unless they are born again” (John 3:3). The new birth is a regenerative work of the Holy Spirit. It is not just a moral or ethical improvement but a complete transformation of one's inner being. This transformation occurs when a person, through faith in Jesus Christ, receives the life of God into their spirit. Being born again is the beginning of a new life. It is not the end, but the start of a lifelong journey of sanctification, where the believer grows more like Christ with each passing day.

When an individual is born again, they are spiritually united with Christ in His death and resurrection. This union is foundational to the Christian's new identity and new life. The old life, bound by sin, is crucified with Christ, and the new life is resurrected with Him. The believer is not only forgiven but also empowered to live a victorious Christian life by the indwelling life of Christ. This new life enables the believer to live in obedience to God's will. A person must believe in the work of Christ—His death, resurrection, and ascension—as the basis for their new life. It is not just about forgiveness or salvation in the sense of an external transaction but about a deep, inward transformation. This new nature comes from God and is not a product of human effort or willpower. The new birth is, therefore, an act of divine mercy, not a result of human decision or effort.

The Natural Man / Old Self / Unregenerate Man

“People who do not have God's Spirit do not accept the things that come from his Spirit. They think these things are foolish. They cannot understand them, because they can only be understood with the Spirit's help.” (1 Corinthians 2:14 ESV)

The natural man is where we all start life, the life inherited from Adam. The natural man is unregenerate man or unsaved man.

This man/woman cannot experience the Kingdom of God. This is a life without spiritual insight. One is considered to be a dead person even while living. “The natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God.”

The Carnal Believer / The Baby Christian

“Or do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived: neither the sexually immoral, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor men who practice homosexuality, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor swindlers will inherit the kingdom of God. And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God.” (1 Corinthians 6:9–11 ESV)

The carnal believer, or baby Christian, is someone who has accepted Christ but has not yet fully matured in their faith. They have been justified, sanctified, and washed by the blood of Jesus Christ (1 Corinthians 6:11), but they still struggle with the flesh, continuing to live according to their old sinful nature rather than the new nature given by the Holy Spirit.

A carnal believer is one who is dominated by their sinful nature and whose actions and attitudes are still heavily influenced by the world. In other words, they have not yet allowed the transformative work of the Holy Spirit to sanctify them in all areas of their life. Though they have been saved, they may still engage in behaviors like those listed in verses 9 and 10, showing that their maturity in Christ is still developing.

A baby Christian is someone who is new to the faith and, like an infant, has not yet grown in spiritual understanding. They may still be learning the basics of the faith and have not yet experienced the full freedom that comes from walking in the Spirit. While they may sincerely desire to live for Christ, they may still struggle with old habits, worldly temptations, and misunderstandings of God’s Word. A baby Christian can be someone who is newly born again or an old believer who is carnal and not growing spiritually as they should. This is primarily caused by living in disobedience to the Bible.

The Spiritual Man / The Mature Child of God

“The spiritual person judges all things, but is himself to be judged by no one.” (1 Corinthians 2:15 ESV)

The ability to “judge all things” does not mean that the spiritual person condemns or criticizes others in a harsh or self-righteous manner. Rather, it refers to having spiritual insight, or discernment, to rightly understand and evaluate situations, ideas, actions, and motives in light of God's truth. This discerning ability is rooted in the wisdom that comes from the Holy Spirit.

The second part of the verse, “but is himself to be judged by no one”, might initially seem puzzling. Does this mean that the spiritual person is above reproach or immune from correction? Absolutely not. The Scriptures make it clear that Christians are accountable to one another, and the body of Christ is meant to correct and encourage one another in love (Matthew 18:15–17; Galatians 6:1).

What Paul is emphasizing is that the spiritual person, having the mind of Christ, cannot be judged or evaluated by worldly standards. The wisdom of God that resides in them is often incomprehensible to those who do not have the Spirit. The natural person (those who are not spiritually discerned) cannot judge the spiritual person’s motives, because they do not understand the underlying spiritual principles guiding them. In this sense, the spiritual man is not answerable to worldly judgment but is answerable to God alone.
